1 How this performance was calculated

Each mix was tested using three Vanguard index funds as stand-ins for the three building blocks, rebalanced once a year with all dividends and interest reinvested:

US stocksVTSMXVanguard Total Stock Market Index Fund
International stocksVGTSXVanguard Total International Stock Index Fund
BondsVBMFXVanguard Total Bond Market Index Fund

Within the stock portion, 70% went to US and 30% international. So the 80/20 mix tested as 56% VTSMX, 24% VGTSX and 20% VBMFX; 60/40 as 42/18/40; and 40/60 as 28/12/60.

These are not the funds in your plan. Broad index funds were used because they have a much longer record than most of your plan's options and cover far more market conditions — the point is to show how a mix of this shape behaves, not to predict any particular fund. Two of the three funds did not exist for the whole period, so the earliest years draw on the underlying index each fund tracks. The figures also do not reflect your plan's fund expenses or any plan administrative fees, which would reduce returns. Past performance is not a guarantee of future results, and no allocation prevents loss.

Your IRA — where it stands

Six funds doing one job

Your IRA holds six American Funds mutual funds. All six are stock funds, and most of them own the same kind of company — large, well-known US businesses. You are paying six management fees for what is largely one bet.

The recommendation

A simple, low-cost portfolio

Our recommendation is to replace all six funds with a simple, low-cost index portfolio — one holding that owns the entire US stock market and one that owns the rest of the world. Same job, a fraction of the cost, and far less to keep track of. The two funds below are an example of how to do that, not the only way; any pair of broad, low-cost total-market index funds would accomplish the same thing.
